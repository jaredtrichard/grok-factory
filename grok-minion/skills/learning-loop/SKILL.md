# Learning loop

The crew learns how the boss operates and gets better at it over time. The lessons live in a boss profile on the shared computer. Every minion reads the parts it needs before it works, and every minion feeds back what it saw.

## Boss profile

On the shared Grok Bot computer: `/home/box/agent-data/grok-minion/boss/`

- `voice.md` — how the boss writes: tone, length, greetings and sign-offs, words and phrases they use, words they cut, formatting, and how this changes by channel (email, Substack, social, texts).
- `habits.md` — how the boss handles things: what they archive, delete, keep, or file where; what counts as obvious junk; how they triage; what they always want to see.
- `preferences.md` — everything else that recurs: scheduling rules, people and their priority, money rules, tools they prefer.

Create the directory and empty files on first use. Each entry is one short line with the date and the evidence it came from (for example `2026-10-07: cuts "I hope this finds you well" from every email (3 drafts)`). Prefer rewriting or pruning an entry over adding a near-duplicate. A lesson seen once is tentative; mark it `(1x)` and firm it up when it repeats.

## Read before you work

Before drafting anything the boss will send or sign, read `voice.md`. Before triaging, archiving, deleting, or filing, read `habits.md`. Read `preferences.md` when the job touches it. Pass the relevant entries to the lab reviewer too, so review checks against them.

## Learn from what the boss does

The best evidence is what the boss changed. After the boss acts on your work, look at the result and record the lesson:

- A draft the boss edited before sending: compare your draft with what was actually sent (the sent email, the published post). Record what they added, cut, or reworded, as a pattern, not a diff.
- A deletion or archive list: record which items the boss kept, deleted, or moved, and what they had in common.
- A send-back or rejection: record the reason in the boss's words.
- A plain approval with no edits: note it only when it confirms a tentative lesson.

Report the lessons to Kevin in one line with the job outcome. Kevin owns the profile: he merges lessons, prunes stale ones, and resolves contradictions (the newer, repeated behavior wins).

## Do not

- Do not store secrets, account numbers, or full message bodies in the profile; record the pattern, not the content
- Do not ask the boss to fill in the profile; learn it from what they do
- Do not treat one edit as a rule
